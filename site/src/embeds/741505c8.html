What the domain taught me, and what it changed

CMS tools are built in-house and rarely shown, so I studied products that each solve part of the job: headless CMSs, video platforms, creative approval tools, and one from outside media, code review on GitHub.

FindingMistakes rarely come from carelessness. They come from structure the editor can't see: an episode under the wrong season, metadata on the wrong level.

→

DecisionI spent longer on the structure than on the screens, so every field shows the level it lives on.

FindingAlmost every field changes by region: title, synopsis, age rating, release date, rights window, even audio and subtitles.

→

DecisionRegion comes first. The editor picks one, and everything under it shows for that region only.

FindingEvery tool lets you comment on a field. None of them turns a rejection into data.

→

DecisionA rejection is a list of reasons, each pinned to a field, categorised and counted.

Field comments exist. A rejection made of reasons that teach the next check does not.
