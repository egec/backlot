    BİLGE, the customs platform of the Turkish Ministry of Trade

    Customs approvals where every change carries a reason

    BİLGE is built from dozens of modules, and I designed across many of them. This page shows one: KLTS, container and port tracking, where shipping agents register containers, customs officers approve them, and managers sign off what changes after approval. I designed every flow from the system specifications and the backend rules behind them, so each screen offers only what the system allows.

    01

    At a glance

    One form, up to four people, each against a deadline

    The problem

    A container form passes through up to four people before it is closed: the agent who files it, the officer who approves it, and the deputy manager and manager who sign off what changes after approval. Each sees a different slice of the same form, and each decision runs against a deadline.

    Decision 1

    One form, four roles

    Every role opens the same form. What changes is what each role can do with it, and the header colour tells them which side they are on.

    Decision 2

    No change without a reason

    Correction, cancellation, update and rejection all ask for a written reason, and the next role reads it before deciding.

    Decision 3

    Registering is binding

    At the ministry’s request the agent’s last button says Register, not Save, and the confirmation shows the registration number and the remaining balance.

    Constraints

    Legal wording on every label, ministry review on changes, and real customs data I can’t show. Every screen on this page is rebuilt from the original designs with placeholder data.

    02

    Working from specs

    Designed from the system specifications, not from a brief

    Every screen started from the system specifications: the analysts’ requirement documents, the regulation and the backend rules behind each field and status. I read them, worked out what each role may do at each status, and designed the interface so it never offers an action the backend would refuse. The specs also defined the domain: the summary declaration a carrier files before arrival, the port’s own records and transit messages. Two screens came straight out of them: one that compares the declaration with the port data, and a container history that puts every event on one timeline.

    04

    The agent files

    General information first, then the containers

    An agent creates the form in two steps: general information, then the containers. Containers are added one by one or in bulk, each with its parts and equipment. Registering the form sends it to the officer’s queue.

    Page A walkthrough: Add Container, Register

    Decisions

    General information first, because every container on the form shares it

    Parts and equipment sit under their container, so nothing is filed against the wrong one

    Register, not Save: once sent, the form is binding

    05

    The officer decides

    Approve, edit or cancel, and every cancel has a reason

    Officers work from a queue of forms waiting for approval. From a form they approve, edit or cancel it, and cancelling asks for a reason. An approved form can still change later through a correction, cancellation or update request, each with its own reason.

    Page B walkthrough: approve selected, reason drawer

    Decisions

    The officer can approve the selected containers and reject the rest in one step, and the confirmation says exactly that

    Reasons open in a side drawer, so the form stays in view while the officer writes

    Editing a form the agent registered warns that the agent’s record will change

    06

    Sign-off up the chain

    Managers decide on the row

    Some changes need a second signature. Time extensions go from the officer to the manager, corrections to the deputy manager, and cancellations to the manager. Each of them works from a list with the request, the reason and its status, and decides on the row.

    Page C walkthrough: approve and reject on the row

    Decisions

    Approve and reject sit on the row with a confirmation, so a manager can clear a queue without opening every form

    The result appears as a message on the same list, not on a new page

    The same three statuses at every level: pending, approved, rejected
