    BİLGE, the customs platform of the Turkish Ministry of Trade

    Tracking every container through Turkish customs, designed from the system specs

    BİLGE is the Ministry of Trade’s customs platform, made of dozens of modules, and I designed across many of them. This page shows KLTS, container and port tracking, designed from the system specifications and backend rules so every screen offers only what the system allows.

    01

    At a glance

    One container, several sources, one form

    The problem

    A container entering a Turkish port is described in several places: the carrier’s summary declaration, the port’s own records and transit messages. The container tracking form ties them together, and up to four people work on it, each against a deadline.

    Decision 1

    One form, four roles

    Agents and customs staff open the same form; what changes is what each can do with it. Blue and red headers separate the agent side from customs.

    Decision 2

    Sources side by side

    The carrier’s declaration and the port’s records sit next to each other, so a mismatch is visible at a glance.

    Decision 3

    Registering is binding

    At the ministry’s request, the agent’s last button says Register. The confirmation shows the registration number and the remaining balance.

    Constraints

    Legal wording on every label, ministry review on every change, and customs data I can’t show.

    02

    Working from specs

    Designed from the system specifications

    Every screen started from the specs: the analysts’ requirements, the regulation and the backend rules behind each field and status. I designed so the interface never offers an action the backend would refuse.

    04

    The agent files

    General information first, then the containers

    The agent fills in general information, then adds containers one by one or in bulk, each with its parts and equipment. Register sends the form to the officer’s queue.

    Page A walkthrough: Add Container, Register

    Decisions

    General information comes first, because every container on the form shares it.

    Parts and equipment sit under their container, so nothing is filed against the wrong one.

    Register, not Save: once sent, the form is binding.

    05

    Tracking a container

    Every source and every event in one place

    Two screens came straight from the specs. One compares the carrier’s summary declaration with the port’s records. The other, the container history, puts every event on one timeline.

    Container lookup (page D) and declaration comparison
    06

    Customs decides

    Officers and managers act from their queue

    Officers approve, edit or cancel registered forms. Some later changes need a second signature: time extensions and cancellations go to the manager, corrections to the deputy manager.

    Pages B and C walkthrough

    Decisions

    An officer can approve some containers on a form and reject the rest in one step.

    Written reasons open in a side drawer, so the form stays in view.

    Managers decide on the row, and the result appears on the same list.

    Editing a form an agent registered warns that the agent’s record will change.
