    BİLGE, the customs platform of the Turkish Ministry of Trade

    Container tracking for Turkish customs

    BİLGE is the Ministry of Trade’s customs platform, made of dozens of modules, and I designed across many of them. This page shows KLTS, container and port tracking, designed from the system specifications and backend rules so every screen offers only what the system allows.

    CompanyMilSOFT
    ClientTurkish Ministry of Trade
    My roleUX/UI Designer
    Date2022 to 2023
    01

    At a glance

    One container, several sources, one form

    The problem

    Every container that enters or leaves a Turkish seaport needs a Container Tracking Form. KLTS moved that form, and the records customs, port operators and agencies used to exchange on paper, into one system.

    Up to four people work on one form, and from registration the container’s permitted time is running.

    Decision 1

    One form, four roles

    Agents and customs staff open the same form; what changes is what each can do with it. Blue and red headers separate the agent side from customs.

    Decision 2

    Sources side by side

    The carrier’s declaration and the port’s records sit next to each other, so a mismatch is visible at a glance.

    Decision 3

    Registering is binding

    Registering starts the clock on the container’s permitted time in the country, so at the ministry’s request the button says Register. The confirmation shows the registration number and the remaining prepaid balance.

    Constraints

    Legal wording on every label, ministry review on every change, and customs data I can’t show.

    02

    Working from specs

    Designed from the system specifications

    Every screen started from the specs: the analysts’ requirements, the regulation and the backend rules behind each field and status. An exit can’t be registered without an entry, approval needs enough prepaid balance, and a container can’t leave the port without an approved form. I designed so the interface never offers an action the backend would refuse.

    My role

    04

    The agent files

    General information first, then the containers

    The agent picks the movement (foreign or domestic, entry or exit), fills in general information, then adds containers one by one or in bulk. Until an officer approves the form, the agent can still edit it.

    1. Every container on the form shares it, so the agent fills it once. Picking a tax ID fills in the company details.
    2. Each container carries its own parts and equipment, so nothing is filed against the wrong one.
    3. The list shows each container’s route, weight and parts before anything is sent.
    4. At the ministry’s request the last button says Register: from that moment the form is binding.
    5. The confirmation gives the registration number, and the form moves to the pending list.
    05

    Tracking a container

    Every source and every event in one place

    Two screens came straight from the specs. The container history puts every event on one timeline. The comparison sets the carrier’s summary declaration against the port’s records.

    1. Gate moves, declarations and form approvals from different systems, newest first.
    2. Opening an event shows its stage, package count, weight and the form it belongs to.
    3. Shortfall lists containers the port recorded but the declaration missed. Surplus lists the reverse.
    4. Selecting a container shows its notice, registration and bill of lading next to the other source.
    06

    Customs decides

    Officers and managers act from their queue

    Officers approve, edit or cancel registered forms; after approval, only customs can change them. Some changes need a second signature: time extensions and cancellations go to the manager, corrections to the deputy manager.

    1. The agent’s details sit above the containers, each with its parts and equipment.
    2. The officer selects the containers that pass. Approving the selection rejects the others in the same step.
    3. Before anything changes, the confirmation says how many containers are approved and how many rejected.
    4. The success message shows how much of the agent’s prepaid balance is left.
    5. Time extensions reach the manager as a list with the request and its reason. Approve and Reject sit on the row, behind a confirmation.
    6. The status changes in place and a message confirms it, so the manager carries on with the queue.

    Also in this flow

    Written reasons open in a side drawer, so the form stays in view.

    Editing a form an agent registered warns that the agent’s record will change.
