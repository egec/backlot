Hi, I’m Ege.

Every job so far began in a domain I didn’t know: customs regulation, naval radar, customer data, maternal and fetal health. That first stretch is my favourite part. Before I draw anything, I want to know how the work is really done, who pays when it goes wrong, and which rule the backend will never bend.

Most of what I need is already written down by someone who isn’t a designer. How I work

01

Learn the domain first

Customs declarations, naval radar displays, customer data, a prenatal monitoring wearable. Each time, I learned how the work is really done, and by whom, before I started designing.

02

Design for the unhappy path

Most products work when everything goes right. I spend more of my time on the other cases: the action that fails, the data that is missing, the step someone skips. That is where people decide whether they can trust a tool.

03

A reason for every decision

If I cannot say why something sits where it does, it moves or goes. Hierarchy, colour and wording all need a reason I could defend in a review.

04

Say the trade-off out loud

Most decisions cost one side something: more control is more surface to maintain, a faster flow means fewer confirmations. I put the trade-off on the table with the PM and the engineers, and when the call goes the other way, I make sure it is written down.

05

The fortieth time, not the first

I look for how people work when they are tired, in a hurry and doing it for the fortieth time today. Support tickets, session replays and watching someone work without stepping in tell me more than the brief.
