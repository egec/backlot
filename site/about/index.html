About

Every project began in a domain I didn’t know: customs regulation, naval radar, customer data, maternal and fetal health. That first stretch is my favourite part. Before I draw anything, I want to know how the work is really done, who pays when it goes wrong, and which rule the backend will never bend.

Most of what I need is already written down by someone who isn’t a designer. So I read the specs, sit with the system engineers, and take a PM’s request apart until I find the customer problem underneath it. When a decision costs one side something, I say so in the room instead of hiding it in the layout. When the call goes the other way, I want it written down, so we can come back to it once real usage shows who was right.

The fastest way to settle those conversations is usually to show something. AI is part of how I work every day: I explore and prototype with Claude, and I built and shipped this site in code with Claude Code, live prototypes included. Often the first thing a PM sees from me is something they can try.

I’m based in Berlin and open to relocation. I’m looking for a team with high standards and bad jokes.

What I like to own
The whole arc of a feature: the first interview, the flows, the prototype, and the review of what developers built against the design.
Working with clients
I bring options to a review instead of arguing for one: the version I recommend, next to the one I expect them to ask for. The client feels heard, the decision comes faster, and I get back to the work with what I need.

How I work

No playbook, no problem

Customs, naval radar, customer data, prenatal health. Each domain was new to me and none came with a playbook. I learned how the work is really done and who does it, then turned messy requests into clear flows.

01.

Design for the unhappy path

Most products work when everything goes right. I spend more of my time on the other cases: the action that fails, the data that is missing, the step someone skips. That is where people decide whether they can trust a tool.

02.

Systems that hold
as the product grows

I’ve extended and maintained design systems at two companies: adding components when a flow needed them and fixing the ones that broke in real use, so the product stays consistent as it changes.

03.

AI that people can check

When a feature uses AI, I design for review: what it suggests, how sure it is, and a quick way to accept, change or reject it. Nothing is written without a person.

04.

Decisions with evidence

Support tickets, session replays and watching people work tell me more than a brief. Every decision needs a reason I can defend, and the trade-off goes on the table.
