Hi, I’m Ege[eh-GEH].

People tell me what they want.
I like to design what they meant.

Every job so far began in a domain I didn’t know: customs regulation, naval radar, customer data, maternal and fetal health. That first stretch is my favourite part. Before I draw anything, I want to know how the work is really done, who pays when it goes wrong, and which rule the backend will never bend.

Most of what I need is already written down by someone who isn’t a designer. So I read the specs, sit with the system engineers, and take a PM’s request apart until I find the customer problem underneath it. When a decision costs one side something, I say so in the room instead of hiding it in the layout. When the call goes the other way, I want it written down, so we can come back to it once real usage shows who was right.

The fastest way to settle those conversations is usually to show something. AI is part of how I work every day: I explore and prototype with Claude, and I built and shipped this site in code with Claude Code, live prototypes included. Often the first thing a PM sees from me is something they can try.

I’m based in Berlin and open to relocation. I’m looking for a team with high standards and bad jokes.

What I like to own
The whole arc of a feature: the first interview, the flows, the prototype, and the review of what developers built against the design.
Working with clients
I bring options to a review instead of arguing for one: the version I recommend, next to the one I expect them to ask for. The client feels heard, the decision comes faster, and I get back to the work with what I need.

How I work

Learn the domain first

Customs declarations, naval radar displays, customer data, a prenatal monitoring wearable. Each time, I learned how the work is really done, and by whom, before I started designing.

01.

Design for the unhappy path

Most products work when everything goes right. I spend more of my time on the other cases: the action that fails, the data that is missing, the step someone skips. That is where people decide whether they can trust a tool.

02.

A reason for every decision

If I cannot say why something sits where it does, it moves or goes. Hierarchy, colour and wording all need a reason I could defend in a review.

03.

Say the trade-off out loud

Most decisions cost one side something: more control is more surface to maintain, a faster flow means fewer confirmations. I put the trade-off on the table with the PM and the engineers, and when the call goes the other way, I make sure it is written down.

04.

The fortieth time, not the first

I look for how people work when they are tired, in a hurry and doing it for the fortieth time today. Support tickets, session replays and watching someone work without stepping in tell me more than the brief.

Things you might ask

Something else? Ask me at a.egecoskun@gmail.com or on LinkedIn.

What’s the hardest workflow you’ve designed?

Backlot, the case study that got me my last job: an editor prepares content, a publisher approves it, and the complexity is in the rules between them. The decision I’d defend anywhere: rejection reasons are a fixed list, not free text. You can’t count free-form comments, and if you can’t count them, you can’t tell an editor what they keep getting wrong.

When did a technical constraint change your design?

At MilSOFT the customs backend was fixed and there was no product brief. Most of the work was separating fields that were written in sequence but weren’t actually dependent, so the form follows what an officer knows at each point. Where the model did force an order, a blocked field says what has to happen first instead of sitting there greyed out.

What do you do when you have to compromise?

Put the trade-off on the table instead of quietly optimising for one side. If the call goes the other way, I ask for it to be written down and design the chosen version as well as I can. Real usage usually settles it later.

How do you use AI?

Every day, for real work: exploring, prototyping, and building this site in code. I still make every decision, and I check what the AI makes against how the system actually behaves, because my assumption about the data is usually the thing that’s wrong.

When can you start?

Within two weeks of signing. I’m based in Berlin and open to relocation.
